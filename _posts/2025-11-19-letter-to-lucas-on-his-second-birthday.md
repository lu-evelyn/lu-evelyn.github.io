Dear Lucas,

You turned two today. A few weeks ago, your flu developed into pneumonia. You spent a week on an IV at the hospital. After you came home, the cough and fever kept coming back, and your grandparents on both sides took you back to the hospital again and again. I rushed back to see you that weekend. You might have been uncomfortable at the hospital, because you cried the whole time we were in the hallway. When we got outside, you looked at me and finally smiled. It was that same pure, happy smile of yours, as if all the sadness from a moment before had vanished. I want to learn that from you. Feel what's happening right now, be honest about how you feel, and let the feeling pass.

You haven't fully recovered yet, so sweets are off the menu, including birthday cake. In a few days I'll make it up to you and take you out for a really delicious cake.

I've been going through a low spell myself. You've been sick for almost a month, and I've been worried about you. I'm also going through a career transition with a lot of uncertainty. Your dad is working abroad, and when I've felt lost or sad, I've often had very little emotional support. So I've spent a lot of time crying on my own lately. I think that once you're grown, I'll want to share with you some of the feelings I find hard to talk about with anyone else. I believe you'll give me a kind of love and support that only you can give.

I'm telling you how I feel because I want us to be able to talk to each other as equals. I've always hoped we could be best friends as well as mother and child. You've only just learned to talk and picked up a little English, but you'll grow so quickly. Before long, you'll start to make sense of the world around you, and you'll be a little source of strength for me.

A few days ago, I wrote down what I wanted my life to look like in ten years. One thing I wrote was, “Be good friends with Lucas.” If I get there in ten years, I believe both of us will be very happy.

It seems every mother has all kinds of hopes for her child. I do too. Money and status are beside the point for me. I hope you'll find something you enjoy and are good at, and that you can earn enough doing it to live the life you want. That's plenty. It's also harder than it sounds. Getting to know yourself is hard and important. Many people go through their whole lives without knowing what they enjoy or what they're good at. They go to school, take exams, choose a major most people think is sensible, and get a job after graduation. That's the world I grew up in. I got lucky and stumbled into something I loved and was good at when I graduated. I hope you find your way to knowing yourself sooner, so you have more time to try things, make mistakes, change course, and grow. I'll help you as much as I can along the way.

I also hope you grow up confident, brave, and steady inside. I hope you grow up surrounded by love, with enough security and ease to explore the kind of life you want. I'll walk alongside you.

Happy birthday!

With unconditional love, always, Mom<br>
November 19, 2025, Beijing
