Dear Lucas,

Time has gone quietly by, and here you are at three, safe and healthy.

You and I can have all sorts of conversations now. You're starting to have ideas of your own and tell me what they are. Your favorite thing to say is “But why?” It makes me laugh.

You're curious about so much in the world. You listen intently while Grandma reads you one book after another.

You have so much empathy. I think you show more of it than most children your age. I first saw it when you were a year and a half old. One day I was sitting at the table eating, and you, barely walking, came over dragging the robe I used to wear all the time and handed it to me. You must have thought I might be cold. That robe was long and heavy, and it hung on the back of a chair in the other room. You had to tug it down and drag it all the way to me. I'll always remember that.

You have a good eye, too. When we go out together, I ask you which shoes and earrings I should wear that day. You really do have good taste.

There's a lot you help heal in me, and you give me so much warm, tender love. Every day when I get home from work and you look up at me, blinking those clear eyes and saying, “Mommy, Mommy,” I feel happy. Pleasure and happiness feel different to me. Pleasure comes from dopamine, and happiness feels like endorphins, a deep, gentle feeling that wells up from within and stays with you.

A lot has changed for me this year, too. I left a job I didn't like and moved into a role that suits me better at this point in my life. I worked through some tensions in the family that had troubled me the previous two years. I started exercising again and have slowly been finding my footing, physically and mentally.

I'm so grateful for everything my parents have done for me. When I was stretched beyond what I could manage, they put their whole hearts into being there with you, teaching you about the world and how to get along in it, and making delicious food for us. They gave both of us all their love. I believe you feel that too.

I wrote you a letter when you turned two, and I'll write you one every year from now on. I didn't write one when you turned one. Today I wondered whether to go back and write that first letter. I realized my state of mind is different now, so whatever I wrote would be different too. Putting an earlier date on it wouldn't help. I prefer writing from the moment I'm actually in.

I do want to use this letter to remember how I felt when you were one.

My body was still very weak after giving birth. Your arrival made family life much more complicated overnight. It used to be just your dad and me. Now there were five people under one roof, with you and either my parents or his. We all had different habits, and there was plenty of friction. My hormones were still swinging wildly after giving birth, and I was in a rough place mentally. I could barely look after myself. That's why I didn't have it in me to write you a letter then.

Having something left in you matters. When your body is well and your mind is at ease, you have the energy to create, to deal with change, and to live well. I hope you learn to look after yourself and take care of that energy.

As hard as that time felt, it's behind me now. The brain has a way of protecting us by slowly letting the painful feelings fade. When I look back, it doesn't seem quite so terrible anymore. I'd rather give my attention to the happiness I feel now and really take it in.

Time is extraordinary, and it has a lot of power. It can soften so many things and change so many others. When something sad happens to you, try not to be afraid. Tell yourself that with time it will pass, and things will get better. When something makes no sense to you, try not to be discouraged. You may simply need more life experience. Give yourself time, and you'll slowly come to understand it.

Change is the one constant in this world. Learn to adapt, to experience things, and to explore. I'll be there to grow and experience things alongside you. I hope we can get to know ourselves and the world together.

With unconditional love, always, Mom<br>
November 19, 2026
